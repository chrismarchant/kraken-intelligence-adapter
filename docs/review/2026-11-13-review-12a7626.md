## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | medium (blocking) | In key-injection mode a request naming only `max_completion_tokens` was accepted, reserved at that figure and forwarded as written, with no `max_tokens`. If the provider ignores that field, each request reserves little and generates up to the provider's default. Probe against a loopback stand-in that honours only `max_tokens`: with a ceiling of 5,000, the account reached 60,000 after two requests. The overshoot is bounded by the concurrency limit times the provider's maximum output. **Whether the real provider ignores the field was not verified** |
| F2 | low (unproven) | The caller's own bytes were forwarded whenever a limit was named, so a repeated JSON key could be read differently by the shim and by the provider |
| F3 | low | Only runs of ordinary spaces were written out; other blanks (no-break, en, em, ideographic) passed, so a stored line could imitate the tool's continuation margin. Nothing reached the first column and nothing was hidden |
| F4 | low (unproven) | With output piped, the width was assumed to be 80; a narrower viewer could wrap stored text into the first column |
| F5 | low | A store writer who deletes a revision can obtain another on the same signed request. Extra model calls only, within the spend account; the approval does not carry over |
| F6 | low (unproven) | A caller that stopped reading a large non-streamed response could hold a concurrency place; no timeout was set |
| F7 | info | Prompt tokens are outside the shim's ceiling; a `select` decision on a draft does not affect what holds a post; a plan rebuilt with identical slots keeps earlier approvals (visible at review); the full listing printed absent and the text "null" alike; a long listing can scroll material away |
